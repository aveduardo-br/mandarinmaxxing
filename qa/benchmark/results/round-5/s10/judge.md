## run-1

**1. Natural Mandarin: 4.** Most answers are natural. Problems:
- T8 Revisão 4, expected 他今天说什么？他是中国人吗？: "What does he say today?" is not something anyone would ask, and the two questions don't connect. Fix: drop 今天 and make it a real exchange, e.g. prompt "Desculpe, o que ele disse? Ele é chinês?" → 对不起，他说什么？他是中国人吗？
- T24 R2.1 昨天她喝茶，今天她喝水 and the narration line 昨天我喝茶: a single finished past event would normally take 了 (昨天她喝了茶). The contrast frame makes it acceptable, and 了 is unmet, but a habit frame would be cleaner (她今天想喝茶，明天想喝水).
- T36 R3.2 昨天我在中国。我不会说中文。今天我想说中文。: grammatical, but the logic is thin ("today I want to speak Chinese"). Acceptable.

**2. Whole sentences: 4.** Every prompt is a whole sentence. Revisão 4 (T8) is a pair nobody would actually say (see above). R2.5 (T32) "O senhor está na China amanhã?" is odd Portuguese for a future ("vai estar").

**3. Round progression: 3.** Round 1 practises the chunks, but R1.3 (T18) "Você sabe falar chinês?" is chunk 2 exactly as its meaning line gives it. That breaks "never ask for a chunk exactly as listed". Round 2 has two prompts that are round 1 style without a when or where: R2.3 (T28) "Quem sabe falar chinês?" and R2.4 (T30) "Onde você estava ontem?". R2.4 is also the frame of Revisão 6 (T12) "Onde você está hoje?" with only the time changed. R3.4 (T40) opens with "Amanhã ela vai à China.", word for word R1.1 (T14). "X sabe falar chinês" comes back with only the subject changed in R1.2, R1.3, R1.4 and R2.3. Round 3 does give three connected sentences (T34–T42).

**4. Only met words: 5.** No `[NÃO SEI]` markers, and every prompt can be answered with met words. Older words 对不起 (T6) and 是 (T8) and the item to reinforce 什么 (T6, T10) are all in the review.

**5. Corrections: 5.** Every correction (T8, T14, T20, T26, T32, T38, T44, T48) has the blockquote, one fix line in bold with pinyin, and the full sentence once on three lines. All are correct and point only at the error, e.g. T32: "**在** (*zài*): 'estar em' é **在**. **去** quer dizer 'ir'". Pinyin answers are confirmed in Hanzi (T10, T18, T34, T42), and the keyboard reminder comes once (T10).

**6. Listening: 4.** The dictation (T46) has four single-turn sentences in one voice, with all five new words across the step. The correction (T48) uses the blockquote and fix line, then the script. The narration is in the first person, never addresses a listener and quotes no one. Problems: it is short, about 12–15 seconds (seven short clauses), below the 20–40 second target even though 46 met words were available. After the learner answered, its script was never shared with pinyin and a translation (T50), which lesson-format.md calls for.

**7. Flow and closing: 5.** The five steps come in order: review with a fixed counter of 6 for 6 words, table, chunks, three rounds, then the offer of another round or listening (T44). The closing (T50) gives the next part, says Lesson 4 is complete and offers the role-play, and offers to start the next part. There is no comment on how the lesson was divided.

**8. Session length.** 23 learner answers plus the table and listening: about 35–40 minutes. That fits a five-word part, and the next part is offered.

**9. Level fit: 3.** The review is mostly first-lesson formulas: "Com licença, de que país você é?" (T2), "Desculpe, o que você está dizendo?" (T6), "Como a senhora se chama?" (T10). The rounds stay at one short clause per sentence, although 46 met words would allow 喜欢/想/也 combinations with time words.

**Verdict.** A learner gets accurate, clean corrections and real practice with 会 and the 中文/汉语 pair. The tutor asks for 汉语 by name every time the learner falls back on 中文, which is exactly what this scenario tests. The weaknesses are variety and level: the same few frames (X 去中国 tomorrow, X 在中国 yesterday, X 会说中文) come back with only the subject changed, a round 1 prompt repeats a chunk as listed, and the review sounds like Lesson 1. **Single change:** tighten the frame rule so a short part must vary its verbs with older words (喝, 吃, 叫, 喜欢) instead of cycling the same frame with a new subject, and so no sentence of an earlier prompt reappears.

## run-2

**1. Natural Mandarin: 4.** Problems:
- T32 R3.1 明天我去中国。你想去吗？我也会说中文。: 也 has nothing to attach to (nobody else was said to speak Chinese). Fix: 我会说中文。 (prompt "Eu sei falar chinês.").
- T26 R2.3 他是中国人，昨天他在中国。: two unrelated clauses joined by a comma. Better: 他是中国人，可是他今天不在中国 (可是 is unmet, so rather two separate prompts).
- T44 dictation 3 你会说汉语，我也想说。: acceptable but elliptical. "我也想说" with no object reads like a half line. Acceptable.
- The narration (T44) is natural: 我不是中国人，也不会说中文。我喜欢中国，也喜欢中国茶。

**2. Whole sentences: 4.** All prompts are sentences. R2.1 (T22) "Senhor, o senhor vai à China amanhã?" is stilted, with a double address. R3.1 (T32) has the non sequitur "também". The added line "Use 汉语 para 'chinês'." under three prompts (T18, T30, T34) is an instruction, not part of the sentence. It is acceptable as the "ask for it by name" step.

**3. Round progression: 3.** Round 1 swaps chunk slots correctly, and no chunk is asked as listed. Round 2 has two prompts with no when or where, which are really round 1 chunk swaps: R2.2 (T24) "Com licença, quem sabe falar chinês?" and R2.5 (T30) "Eu também sei falar chinês.". Round 3 has three-sentence prompts (T32–T40), but some are lists rather than connected accounts (R3.3, T36: yesterday tea, today food, tomorrow water).

**4. Only met words: 4.** No `[NÃO SEI]` markers. R2.1 (T22) invites the unmet 先生 through its vocative. Otherwise everything can be answered with met words. 对不起 (T4) and 是 (T2) are in the review, and 什么 comes in T4.

**5. Corrections: 4.** The format is right in T8, T20, T32 and T38. T14's fix line repeats the whole sentence (**明天他去中国**) instead of the stretch (**明天** before the verb), so the sentence appears twice. T26 accepts 谁说中文 for "quem sabe falar chinês" although leaving out 会 changes the meaning. It should have been a correction, and the learner's pinyin answer was never shown back in Hanzi. The dictation fix (T46) and the narration marking (T48) are correct.

**6. Listening: 5.** The dictation (T44) has four single-turn sentences, and the 汉语 line catches the 中文 habit (T46 corrects it clearly). The narration has six first-person sentences, about 20 seconds, in a new scene, with no listener addressed and no quotes. The script is shared with pinyin and meaning after the answers (T48). All five new words appear across the step. Minor: question 3 ("isso quer dizer que ele não quer… ou não sabe…?") is a leading two-option grammar question rather than a comprehension question.

**7. Flow and closing: 5.** The steps come in order, round 3 is followed by the offer of another round or listening (T42), and the closing (T48) gives the next part, the role-play offer and the offer to start now. Minor: the review has 5 prompts for 6 words (哪 and 国 share T2), where the skill asks for one prompt per word.

**8. Session length.** 21 learner answers plus the table and listening: about 35–40 minutes. That fits, and the next part is offered.

**9. Level fit: 3.** The review is short and formulaic ("Ele é de que país?" T2, "Com licença, onde ela está?" T6). Only Revisão 5 (T10) reaches round 2 length. The rounds stay short for a learner with 46 words.

**Verdict.** A learner gets solid practice with 会, 明天 and 昨天, and the most insistent handling of 汉语: required by name three times from the first 中文 choice, then planted in the dictation. The listening step is the stronger of the two runs. The weak points are a vocative in a prompt that the answer drops (T22) and accepting an answer without the new word 会 when that changes the meaning (T26), plus the same frame repetition and low review level as run-1. **Single change:** in lesson-format.md, say that when leaving out the new word changes the meaning, the answer is incorrect and gets the correction format, not "name it and bring it back".

## Cross-run summary

**In both runs (points at the instructions):**
- Frames repeat with only the subject or time changed: "X 明天去中国", "X 昨天在中国", "X 会说中文" across rounds 1–3. This breaks SKILL.md's no-shared-frame rule. With five new words that all fit a "China/language" scene, both tutors cycled the same three frames instead of mixing in older verbs.
- The review sits at first-lesson level (formulas such as 你是哪国人, 你叫什么名字, 你说什么), not at round 2 level.
- Round 2 contains chunk swaps with no when or where ("Quem sabe falar chinês?" in both runs).
- Handled well in both runs: 汉语 required by name three times whenever the learner chose 中文, every older word due was used in the review, no unmet words in hints or scripts, and the closing is complete.

**Seen once:**
- run-1: a chunk asked exactly as listed (R1.3); a round 1 sentence repeated word for word in round 3 (R1.1 / R3.4); a narration below 20 s whose script was never shared after the answers; the incoherent review pair 他今天说什么？他是中国人吗？
- run-2: the vocative "Senhor," the answer drops (T22); 谁说中文 accepted without 会 (T26); 5 review prompts for 6 words; 也 with no antecedent (R3.1); the fix line repeats the whole sentence (T14).

**Three changes to make first:**
1. SKILL.md, line 186: "In the whole part, the review included, no two prompts share the same frame with only the subject, the time, the place or a name swapped". Add: "No sentence of an earlier prompt reappears in a later one. When the new words all fit one scene, vary the frame with older verbs and objects from the **Done** log (喝, 吃, 叫, 喜欢) rather than reusing the same verb with a new subject."
2. SKILL.md, line 177: "Write each at the level of the course, not of the part reviewed: a sentence as long as round 2's". Add: "A set formula on its own (你是哪国人, 你叫什么名字, 你说什么) is below that level: put the word in a statement with a time or place and a second clause."
