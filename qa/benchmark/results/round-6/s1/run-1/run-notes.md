## Places where the skill was unclear or hard to apply

1. **Language of the welcome.** `setup.md`: "Write to the learner in the language they ask for, or the one they are writing in. With too little text to tell, use what the interface or what you know about them points to, or ask in one short line first." The only text was `start mandarinmaxxing`, which is the trigger phrase `setup.md` itself tells learners to type, so it says nothing about their language. I asked in one line (T2), and the student answered "Português." (T3). That adds one exchange the scenario does not list.

2. **Round 1 with three words (part 1).** These rules cannot all hold together with 你好 再见 谢谢:
   - "Each prompt uses one of the part's chunks with a slot changed … never the chunk or its example as listed." With three one-word expressions, a chunk has no slot. Prompt 1 (你好！) is a chunk as listed.
   - "Every new word still appears in at least three prompts." versus "When the met words can't make five different natural prompts, give fewer. Never repeat a word inside a prompt, echo a line … or reshuffle lines already used." Three appearances of 你好 without an echo need the three-word line 你好！谢谢！再见！ (prompt 5), which is close to "reshuffling" prompts 3 and 4. I kept five prompts and three appearances per word; a stricter reading of "give fewer" would have stopped at four prompts with two appearances of 你好 and 再见.
   - "two to four useful chunks": I listed two (你好！ and 谢谢！), which are just two of the table's words. Any multi-word chunk would have used up one of the few possible prompts.

3. **Dictation with three words (part 1).** `lesson-format.md`: "No sentence repeats a chunk or a prompt of the part" and "Across this step, use every new word", with "With very few met words, two or three real lines are enough." Every natural line made of these three words had already been a chunk or a prompt, so the dictation (你好！/ 谢谢！/ 再见！) repeats them. I could not satisfy both sentences.

4. **Review of part 2.** "One prompt per new word of the last Done line, five to ten in all … If the last part had fewer than five words, fill the review with the items to reinforce and the older words, each in a prompt of its own" and "sentences as long as round 2's". With three met words, one item to reinforce (再见, which is also one of the three words) and no older words, I gave four one- or two-word prompts ("Revisão 1 de 4"), relying on "The same goes for the review and the dictation" (give fewer). It is unclear whether "Met words … this part's table" lets the review use the part's new words before the table is shown; I assumed not.

5. **No offer after the last round.** "After round 3, offer one more round like it or the listening step, and wait." Part 1 had round 1 only and part 2 skipped round 3 ("Skip round 3"), and the skill doesn't say whether the offer still applies after the last round given. I went straight to the dictation in both parts.

6. **Narration.** "Only once the met words can fill about 20 seconds; before that the dictation is the whole step." With nine met words, all of them greetings and reactions, a first-person narration isn't possible, so both parts had the dictation only.

7. **Round 2 as exchanges versus "one sentence".** For six to nine met words the skill asks for "a round 2 of short exchanges in which the second line reacts to the first", while the saved round name is "Ações completas". I kept the label and added one line of instruction at T40 on how to write the two lines. "Say 'Translate into Mandarin' … nowhere else" made me word that line without the phrase.

8. **没关系 and 没事.** "give each prompt a context that fits only one, or accept both." I used two fixed glosses ("não tem problema" / "não foi nada") so each prompt points to one, and would have accepted either; the student never swapped them, so this wasn't tested.

9. **To reinforce: when to add.** "After a correction, bring the item back … and add it to the words to reinforce" versus "After each part, remove the items the learner got right unaided during the part, then add the ones they missed and didn't get right later." I applied the second at the close: items corrected and then written right later in the same part were not saved.

10. **To reinforce after the role-play.** `requests.md` says a request "leaves Next and Done as they are" and that each reply is corrected "as a written translation", but not whether a role-play mistake goes into **To reinforce**. I added 不客气 (missed at T59, no later turn to get it right).

11. **Role-play format.** "write in Mandarin with the meaning under each line": it doesn't mention pinyin or scene cues. I used the three-line form (Hanzi, pinyin, meaning) and one plain sentence of scene before each line, since with these words the learner can't tell from 谢谢！ alone why they are being thanked. I gave four turns; a fifth would have repeated a reaction already used.

12. **Voice saved at setup or at the first clip.** `setup.md`: "Save the choice in Audio"; `lesson-format.md`: "On the first clip of a course, choose the voice and save it in Audio". I saved `ElevenLabs` at setup and added `voice Sarah` at the first clip.
