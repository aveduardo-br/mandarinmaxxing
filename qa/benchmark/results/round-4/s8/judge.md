## Run 2

### 1. Natural Mandarin: 3
Grammatical throughout, but several expected answers sound off:
- T32, Round 2 · 5: 那位先生是我爷爷，他正高兴地做饭。 Calling your own grandfather 那位先生 is odd, since 位 and 先生 are honorifics for other people. 正高兴地做饭 is also stiff. Fix: **那个人是我爷爷，他正在做饭，很高兴。** (or **…他高高兴兴地做饭**).
- T42, Round 3 · 5: 我给妈妈介绍了三位朋友。 Introducing your own friends to your mother with 位 is over-polite. Fix: **我给妈妈介绍了三个朋友。**
- T40, Round 3 · 4: 我丈夫想给我介绍他的朋友。 The prompt says "um amigo dele", so: **我丈夫想给我介绍他的一个朋友。**
- T8, Review 4: 坐出租车，从我家到医院很快。 Better: **从我家到医院，坐出租车很快。**
- T10, Review 5 expected answer: …可是他们做菜很慢。 可是 is HSK 3 (see dimension 4). For a restaurant, 上菜很慢 is what people say, but 做菜很慢 is acceptable with met words.
- T12, Review 6: 妈妈高兴地告诉我，明天给我买新衣服。 Fine, though **她明天要给我买新衣服** is more natural.

The review is at the right level (T4 "O meu irmão mais velho está sentado vendo televisão, e eu vou ajudar a minha mãe a fazer o jantar."; T10 "Eu já estive naquele restaurante. Rounds 1 and 2 are mostly six- to eight-character single clauses, well below what the met words allow. The review is harder than round 2, the reverse of what the skill wants.

### Verdict
A learner gets a usable part with a well-pitched review, accurate corrections that recycle the word-order slip (在…工作), and a clean dictation and narration. But the chunks for 丈夫 and 爷爷 are just HSK 1 sentence frames, so the first two rounds feel like a beginner lesson, and several expected answers misuse the polite 位 (那位先生是我爷爷, 三位朋友). The single most useful change would be to require each chunk to carry the pattern a new word actually needs (给 + person + 介绍, 姓 + surname, 这位/那位 for someone else), not a sentence the new noun merely fills.

## Cross-run summary

**Problems in both runs** (these point at the instructions):
- **Repeated frames.** Run 1: Round 1 · 1 and 1 · 5 use the same 我给X介绍一下，这位是我Y, and Round 1 · 3 and Round 2 · 2 share 不知道…姓什么. Run 2: 在学校/公司/医院工作 three times, and 从我家到… in two consecutive review prompts. The rule exists, but tutors don't apply it to the listed chunk, to reworked prompts after a correction, or to the review.
- **Level drops in places.** Run 1's Review 6 is a short clause. Run 2's rounds 1 and 2 are beginner-length. The level rule covers only the review, so the rounds can fall below it.
- **The polite classifier 位 used where it doesn't fit.** Run 2 has 那位先生是我爷爷 and 三位朋友 for one's own friends. Run 1's 这位是我丈夫 is acceptable. The usage note teaches 位 as "polite for people" without saying for whom.

**Problems seen once:**
- Run 1: the round-1 prompt that is the chunk with a vocative added; the narration whose timeline doesn't hold (three-hour flight there and back the same afternoon) and its 要三个小时 in a past account.
- Run 2: an HSK 3 word (可是) in an expected answer and named in feedback; a two-sentence round-2 prompt; a redundant restatement of the wrong answer in Hanzi (T26); narration questions placed after the clip and no chunk-in-context question; chunks that don't carry the new words' patterns.

**Three changes to make first:**
1. `SKILL.md`, round rules: "In the whole part, no two prompts share the same frame with only the subject or the time swapped." Change to: "In the whole part, including the review and the chunks as listed, no two prompts share the same frame with only the subject, time, place or a vocative changed. When a prompt brings back an item after a correction, put it in a new frame."
2. `SKILL.md`, step 2: "then two to four useful chunks." Change to: "then two to four useful chunks, each built on the pattern a new word needs (给 + person + 介绍一下, 姓 + surname, 这位/那位 + person), not an older sentence frame with the new word dropped in." Pair it with a round-level line under step 3: "Rounds 1 and 2 are never shorter than the review: once the learner has met about 150 words, each prompt adds a time, a place or a second clause."
3. `lesson-format.md`, usage note: "Give a polite form a meaning that says who it is for, such as 您 = “you (to sir or madam)”, so a prompt can call for it." Add: "The same goes for 位: give its meaning as 'polite measure word for someone you're introducing or speaking of with respect', and don't use it for one's own family or friends in plain statements (那个人是我爷爷, 三个朋友)."
