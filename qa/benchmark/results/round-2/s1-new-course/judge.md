## Run 1

**1. Natural Mandarin: 4.** Nearly everything is natural mainland usage (你好！— 你好！, 谢谢，再见！— 再见！, 您好！— 你好！, 好，谢谢您！). Issues:
- T60 dictation: "没关系，没事。" One speaker stacking two near-synonyms reads as padding. Fix: **没事，没事。** or **没关系，没关系。** (doubling one of them is the natural reassurance).
- T40 table: 不客气 given as *bù kèqi*. The syllabus spells it *bú kèqi*. Not wrong, but inconsistent with the skill's own source.
- T64 role-play: meaning of 您好 given as plain "Olá!" with no "(para senhor ou senhora)", which hides why 您 is used.

**2. Whole sentences: 5.** Every prompt is a real line or a dashed exchange: "Bom dia, senhor!" (T40), "Obrigada, senhora! — De nada!" (T52), "Desculpe! — Não foi nada!" (T50). No fragments, no scene descriptions in the prompts. (The role-play has stage directions in parentheses, T66 "*(Ela esbarra na sacola…)*", but that is outside the review/round prompt rule.)

**3. Round progression: 4.** With under ten met words, round 1 + round 2 of exchanges and no round 3 is correct, in both parts. Part 2's round 2 mostly has the second line reacting to the first (T50, T52, T54). Problems: part 1's round 2 is all mirrors that repeat a word inside the prompt ("Olá! — Olá!" T16, "Tchau! — Tchau!" T20), against "Never repeat a word inside a prompt". T24 "Olá! — Olá! — Obrigada, tchau! — Tchau!" and T58 "Bom dia! — Bom dia! — Obrigada! — De nada!" are stitched together from earlier prompts. With three words, part 1 has almost nothing else to offer (see the cross-run summary).

**4. Only met words: 5.** 0 prompts need an unmet word. No `[NÃO SEI]` markers. "Boa tarde!"/"Bom dia!" map onto 你好, which the learner has met.

**5. Corrections: 5.** Every correction follows the format: "Você escreveu:", the blockquote, one line on the fix, then the full sentence once (T12, T18, T42, T46, T54, T60). Pinyin answers are confirmed with Hanzi ("Certo. Em Hanzi: **不客气！**" T50). The keyboard reminder comes once, at the first pinyin answer (T14). The valid alternative 没关系 is acknowledged (T52). After the Review 2 miss (T34), Review 5 is turned into another 你好 prompt.

**6. Listening: 5.** Dictation only, which is right with this few words: 4 sentences (T26), then 5 (T60), one speaker, single-turn lines. Each wrong sentence gets a correction in the format and the script follows with pinyin and meaning (T28, T62). No narration, correctly.

**7. Flow and closing: 5.** Part 1 opens straight on the table (nothing to review). Part 2 has review (5 prompts, T30–T39), table, rounds, dictation, close. The part 1 close names the next part and offers it now (T28). The part 2 close says Lesson 1 is complete, offers the role-play, and offers to start the next part (T62). The role-play has 4 turns with blocks from both parts, and each reply is corrected (T70). No comment on how the lesson was divided.

**8. Session length: ~35 min** (part 1 ~12, part 2 ~18, role-play ~5). That's short of an hour, but these are jump-start parts and each close offers the next part. Fits.

**Verdict.** A learner gets a clean, well-corrected first lesson: greetings, thanks and apologies, the 您 distinction, and 没关系 vs 不客气, each practised several times with good feedback. The weak spot is part 1's round 2, which can only echo three words back ("Olá! — Olá!") and pile them into four-line combos. The skill change that would help most: give the very first part (fewer than about five met words) its own rule for round 2, so it doesn't make filler mirror exchanges.

## Run 2

**1. Natural Mandarin: 4.** Mostly natural. Issues:
- T18 prompt "Obrigada! — Eu que agradeço!" with the expected answer 谢谢！谢谢！. Answering thanks with a bare 谢谢 isn't how "Eu que agradeço" is said. Natural: **也谢谢你！** (uses unmet 也). With only met words, drop the prompt.
- Expected answers and corrections run both speakers together with no dash: "**你好！你好！谢谢，再见！**" (T24), "**您好！你好！谢谢！不客气！**" (T52). On the page it reads like one speaker. Fix: keep the dash in the Hanzi line, as in the meaning line.

**2. Whole sentences: 4.** The lines are real lines, but "Tudo bem!" (T40) on its own doesn't make clear that it answers an apology (it could be 好), so the expected 没关系 rests on the table, not on the sentence. Fix: "Desculpe! — Tudo bem!" "Obrigada! — Eu que agradeço!" (T18) is a sentence the learner can't really translate with what they know.

**3. Round progression: 3.** Correct structure: round 1 + round 2 of exchanges, no round 3. But part 1's round 2 is all mirrors with repeated words ("Olá! — Oi!" → 你好！你好！ T16, "Tchau! — Até logo!" T20) plus one recombination ("Olá! — Oi! — Obrigada, tchau!" T22). In part 2's round 2, 4 of 5 prompts are 3–4 line chains (T50, T52, T54, T56), and T56 "Obrigada! — De nada! — Desculpe! — Tudo bem!" runs thanks before an apology with nothing linking them, which is shuffling lines to make a new prompt.

**4. Only met words: 5.** 0 prompts need an unmet word (T18 stretches meaning but can be answered with 谢谢). No `[NÃO SEI]` markers.

**5. Corrections: 4.** The format is followed throughout (T12, T18, T42, T48, T54, T62, T72), and pinyin is always confirmed with Hanzi (T14, T22, T44, T52). The alternative 没关系 is handled exactly as the format asks: accepted, the target named, brought back later (T50 → T56). One reason is overstated. At T24 the learner wrote 再见，谢谢 and was told "o agradecimento vem antes da despedida", but 再见，谢谢 is also said. The real reason is that the prompt asked for "Obrigada, tchau". The role-play nudge from 你好 to 您好 for an elderly lady (T66) correctly uses the "correct meaning, unnatural phrasing" pattern.

**6. Listening: 5.** Dictation alone in both parts. T28 has 4 sentences and T60 has 5, single speaker, one turn each. Corrections are in the format and the full script follows with pinyin and meaning. No narration, correctly.

**7. Flow and closing: 5.** The steps come in order. The part 2 review has 3 prompts (T32–T37). Strictly, the skill asks for at least five, but the pool here (3 words of part 1, the reinforce item 再见, no older lines) only gives three without repeating, and the skill doesn't say what to do then. After the Review 2 miss, Review 3 is reworked to carry 再见 (T36), which is correct. After round 2 the tutor offers one more round or the listening step (T26, T58). The skill only describes that offer after round 3, but it's harmless. The closes are right: next part plus an offer to start now (T30), and lesson complete plus the role-play offer plus the next part (T62). The role-play has 5 turns covering both parts.

**8. Session length: ~33 min** (part 1 ~12, part 2 ~16, role-play ~5). That fits a jump start, with the next part offered each time.

**Verdict.** The learner gets the same core as run 1, with good corrections and a pleasant role-play, but the exchange rounds feel padded. Many prompts are echoes ("Olá! — Oi!") or four-line chains of earlier lines, and dropping the dash in the Hanzi makes the expected answers harder to read. The skill change that would help most: cap a round-2 exchange at two lines from two speakers, and forbid recombining earlier prompts.

**2. Whole sentences: 4.** Real lines throughout ("Olá, senhora! Desculpe!" T44, "Obrigada, senhora! — De nada! Tchau!" T52). Two weak ones: "Oi! Obrigada! — Tchau!" (T20, see above) and the mirror "Tchau! — Até logo!" (T22).

**3. Round progression: 4.** Correct structure in both parts. Part 2's round 2 is the best of the three runs: every prompt is a short encounter where the second speaker reacts ("Olá, senhora! Desculpe! — Olá! Não foi nada!" T46, "Desculpe! — Não foi nada! Tchau!" T54). Part 1's round 2 has the same forced mirrors as the other runs ("Olá! — Oi!" T16, "Tchau! — Até logo!" T22).

**4. Only met words: 5.** 0 prompts need an unmet word. No `[NÃO SEI]` markers.

**5. Corrections: 5.** The format is followed (T12, T18, T24, T40, T46, T52, T58, T68). Pinyin is confirmed with Hanzi (T14, T42, T50, T62), and the keyboard reminder comes once (T14). The reasons are precise: T40 separates 没关系 from 对不起, and T58 says "os dois são parecidos, mas o som é outro".

**6. Listening: 4.** Dictation alone, 4 and 5 single-turn sentences, one speaker. The script is shown after the answer. But both scripts are compact one-line lists ("1. **你好！** *nǐ hǎo* Olá!" **7. Flow and closing: 4.** The steps come in order, and the closes are correct (T28; T58 has lesson complete, role-play offer, next part offer). Problems: the part 2 review has 3 prompts, and after the Review 2 miss on 再见 (T34) Review 3 was not reworked to bring 再见 back. It stayed "Obrigado!", against "rework a prompt still to come so it uses the item that went wrong". In part 2 the table has no title line above it (T36), though the format asks for the title to repeat at the table when a part opens with review.

**8. Session length: ~30 min** (part 1 ~11, part 2 ~15, role-play ~4). That fits a jump start, with the next part offered each time.

**Verdict.** The learner gets a tight, accurate Lesson 1. Part 2's exchange round is the most natural of the three runs, and the corrections are crisp. They lose a little in the review, which doesn't recycle the missed word, in script displays that break the three-line pattern, and in a role-play whose thanks and apology come out of nowhere. The skill change that would help most: say outright in the review rule that the reworked prompt must come right after the miss, and that a role-play line needs a situation that motivates it.

## Cross-run summary

**Problems in more than one run (these point at the instructions):**
- **Part 1's round 2 is filler in all three runs.** Only 你好 再见 谢谢 are met, so the "second line reacts to the first" round turns into mirrors that repeat a word ("Olá! — Olá!", "Tchau! — Até logo!") and recombinations of earlier prompts (run 1 T24, run 2 T22, run 3 T20). This conflicts with "Never repeat a word inside a prompt or shuffle lines just to make a new prompt."
- **Long exchange chains in part 2's round 2** (runs 1 and 2): 3–4 line prompts made by stringing earlier exchanges together (run 1 T58, run 2 T52–T56).
- **Review shorter than five** (runs 2 and 3: 3 prompts) or padded with duplicates to reach five (run 1: 你好 asked twice, T32 and T38). The skill requires five but gives no rule when the pool is smaller.
- **好 never gets a prompt of its own** in any run's rounds. It only appears inside 您好/你好 (run 1 uses it once, in the dictation). That arguably misses "every new word appears in at least three prompts".
- **Markdown table instead of the HTML template** in all three runs. This isn't scorable from the transcripts, but it's consistent enough to be worth checking.

**Problems seen only once:** run 1's stacked "没关系，没事。" in the dictation and its *bù kèqi* spelling. Run 2's untranslatable "Eu que agradeço!" prompt, the dash dropped in the Hanzi answers, the overstated word-order reason (T24), and the extra-round offer after round 2. Run 3's missing rework after a review miss, compact script display, unmotivated role-play lines, and missing table title in part 2.

**Three changes to make first:**

1. `SKILL.md`, the line "**While the learner has met fewer than ten words**, … Give round 1, then a round 2 of short exchanges in which the second line reacts to the first (thanks → you're welcome, sorry → no problem), and skip round 3." Add: "An exchange has two lines, one per speaker, and never strings earlier prompts together. When the part's words allow no true reaction (the first part of a course), a mirrored greeting or farewell (你好！— 你好！) is the one allowed repeat; if fewer than five such exchanges exist, end round 2 early rather than padding it."
2. `SKILL.md`, the review rule "One prompt per new word of the previous part in the **Done** log, at least five and at most ten. When that part had fewer than five words, fill the review with the items to reinforce and the older words…". Add: "If those together give fewer than five items, the review has one prompt per item and no item is asked twice. After a miss, the very next review prompt uses the missed item."
3. `SKILL.md`, "Across the rounds, every new word appears in at least three prompts." Add: "A word counts only where it stands on its own; a word that also appears inside a fixed chunk (好 in 您好) needs prompts where it is used alone (好，谢谢！ / 好，再见！)."
