# The first parts of a course

Read this when the brief says the part is an early one: the learner has met fewer than twenty words. Everything in `SKILL.md` still holds except what this page changes.

With so few words, rounds of full sentences can't be filled, and a line asked three times is the same exercise three times. So these parts are short, ten to twenty minutes, and practise each line in more than one way. The count of three prompts per word doesn't apply (the plan's `EARLY` line tells the checker).

## Three kinds of exercise

Two more kinds join the translation:

- **Meaning.** Show a line in Mandarin, with its pinyin, and ask what it means.
- **Reply.** Show what someone says, in Mandarin with its meaning, and ask what the learner answers, in Mandarin (谢谢！ → 不客气。). When more than one reply is natural, say which way to answer (accept, refuse).

A line is asked once at most in each kind, so it can come back as meaning, as a reply and as a translation, never twice the same way.

## The steps

- **Review.** Ask the last part's lines in a kind they weren't asked in, or inside a new exchange with words met since, as replies and translations more than as meanings: the learner should write Mandarin. It can have fewer prompts than that part had words.
- **Vocabulary.** As usual. An exchange of two lines can be listed as a chunk, and a chunk with no slot to change is asked as listed.
- **Round 1** uses meaning and reply exercises. **Round 2** asks for translations: one speaker's line, or a short exchange in which the second line reacts to the first, with a dash before each speaker. **Round 3** is given only when the words make two connected sentences; otherwise go from round 2 to the last step. Each round has one exercise per new word, five at most, and fewer when the lines run out. Open these rounds with their number alone (**Round 1**): the names in **Rounds** describe the usual rounds. Don't offer an extra round.
- **Last step.** With audio, a dictation of two or three real lines, which may be lines the part already used. Without audio, skip the reading text: under the reading title, name two to four moments of the lesson's situation in the learner's language (you arrive, someone hands you something, you leave) and ask what they would say in each, in Mandarin.

## Lines people really say

- Lines follow a real encounter: greet, then thank or apologize, then say goodbye.
- One speaker's line holds one reaction (谢谢您！), or two that people really say together (好，再见！): never a greeting glued to an apology or to thanks (你好，对不起！), never two words that mean the same (没事，没关系。). A greeting or a goodbye answered with the same word is a real exchange (你好！ — 你好！) and can be asked once as a reply; nothing else is echoed.

From twenty met words on, the usual rounds apply and every prompt of rounds 1 and 2 is one speaker and one sentence.
