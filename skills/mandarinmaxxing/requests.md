# Requests: anything other than the next part

Read this when the learner asks for something other than the next part. Unless a request says otherwise, it leaves **Next** and **Done** as they are.

## Another lesson

- **Practising a lesson or part** ("let me practise Lesson 9 again"). Teach it from its start as standalone practice: **Next** and **Done** stay as they are.
- **Changing the route.** When the learner jumps to a lesson out of route order ("go to Lesson 22", "start at 19"), the lesson keeps its number. Unless they say otherwise, the lessons jumped over follow it on the route, in map order. Confirm that in one line, save the route, and set **Next** to the first part of that lesson.

## Skip lessons the learner already knows

Where to start is the learner's choice: the first lesson, the middle of the jump start, or any lesson after it. When a jump leaves jump-start lessons unstudied, name those lessons, with a few of the words each one teaches, and ask once which of them the learner already knows.

- **The ones they know:** mark each of those lessons `(known)` in the **Lessons** list and take it off the route. Do the same for any other lesson the learner says they know. Their words count as met, and `SKILL.md` says how to remind the learner of them.
- **The ones they don't:** those jump-start lessons come first, before the lesson they asked for: the later lessons are built on their words.

A lesson marked `(known)` can still be studied later: remove the mark, count its parts again, and put it on the route, first if the learner wants it now and otherwise after the lesson in progress. A lesson already begun stays begun: its **Done** lines show which part comes next when the route returns to it.

## Review a lesson

Use the lesson's lines in the **Done** log: a review-style set of prompts for its words, at the level of the course, one prompt per word and ten at most; in a longer lesson, take the words by turn across its lines. For extra practice on one point, give more prompts on it during the lesson.

## Change the pace

Save the new **Pace**. It takes effect at the next lesson: a lesson already begun finishes at the old pace, so the parts in **Done** keep their numbers. Until it finishes, keep the old pace on the same line, such as `- Pace: about 7 words per part (Lesson 05 finishes at 5)`. Recount the parts of the lessons not yet started in the **Lessons** list.

## Change the audio

Save the new **Audio**. Turning it on follows "Audio" in `setup.md`; turning it off takes effect at the next listening step.

## Change the level

Changing level replaces the route, so confirm it first in one line: the course moves to the new level, and the words already studied keep coming back. When the learner confirms, follow steps 1 to 4 of "When a level ends" in `levels.md`, with one difference: **Next** goes to the lesson they choose from the overview. Words of the levels below the new one count as met.

## The course screen

When the learner writes "setup" or "/setup", read `setup.md` and send the course screen described at its end.

## Back after a break

When the learner says they have been away for weeks, offer a review of the last two or three lessons studied, then go on from **Next**. They decide.

## Wrap-up role-play

Offered once, in the close of the last part of a lesson with two or more parts. When the learner accepts, now or in a later chat, set the scene in one line in their language, then play the other person in the lesson's situation: write in Mandarin with the meaning under each line, and let the learner reply in Mandarin, for four to six turns that call on blocks from every part of the lesson. Accept any natural reply, not only the one you expected, and correct the rest as a written translation. Close it in one line, save any slip in **To reinforce**, and offer the next part. If the learner moves on instead, don't bring it up again.

## Extra reading

A short text in the reading format of `lesson-format.md`, written only with met words, with a rewrite task.

## Flashcards

The words of the parts asked for, and their chunks, in one code block with one card per line: Hanzi, a tab, pinyin, a tab, meaning in the learner's language. Say in one line that it can be saved as a text file and imported into Anki or another flashcard app.
